    About the project

    Lokdhara is a digital database that documents, preserves and promotes India's ancient folk traditions — bringing dances, music, instruments, festivals, crafts, oral traditions and traditional knowledge together on one open, structured platform.

    Vision

    An India where every child can hear the songs of their grandparents, every artisan earns with dignity, and every village tradition has a secure, celebrated place in the nation's memory.

    Mission

    To map, record and share India's folk heritage state by state and district by district, working hand in hand with communities, researchers and institutions so that knowledge flows freely to the next generation.

    Objectives

    1. Document comprehensively. Build a structured record of folk dances, music, instruments, festivals, arts and crafts, oral traditions and traditional knowledge from all 28 states and 8 union territories.
    2. Preserve for generations. Create lasting digital archives of stories, songs, images and practices using open, durable formats.
    3. Promote with pride. Bring folk traditions to classrooms, screens and public life so that more people discover, enjoy and practise them.
    4. Empower tradition bearers. Give artists, artisans and communities visibility, credit and new opportunities for livelihood.
    5. Strengthen research. Offer clean, comparable data for statistical analysis, cultural mapping and policy planning.
    6. Encourage participation. Invite students, surveyors and citizens to contribute knowledge through field surveys and community validation.
    7. Celebrate diversity and unity. Show how many languages, faiths and landscapes together form one shared cultural heritage.

    How Lokdhara works

    1. Cultural mapping. Each tradition is linked to its state, districts and localities so it can be explored on the India map.
    2. Field surveys. Surveyors record practitioners, transmission methods and community needs with a standard form.
    3. Classification. Every entry is tagged by category, historical period and vitality status for consistent analysis.
    4. Statistical analysis. Dashboards reveal patterns by state, category, period and status.
    5. Digital archiving. Records receive accession numbers and are grouped by media type for long-term access.
    6. Community validation. Tradition bearers review and enrich entries, keeping the database accurate and respectful.

      A note on the data

      Entries summarise widely published information and are written to be accurate, respectful and positive. Periods indicate the earliest widely accepted origins, and vitality labels reflect general public knowledge rather than a formal census. Lokdhara grows stronger with every correction and contribution — practitioners and scholars are invited to help refine each record through the Contribute page.

      Frequently asked

      Who can contribute?

      Anyone — students, teachers, artists, artisans, researchers and families who want to share their heritage.

      Will I receive credit?

      Yes. Contributors and tradition bearers are credited on each record they help build.

      Can I use Lokdhara data for research?

      Yes. Export the database from the Cultural Database page and cite Lokdhara along with the original sources.

      How is consent handled?

      Media and personal details are published only with the informed consent of the community and individuals involved.
